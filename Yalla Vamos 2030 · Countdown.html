The essentials

24thFIFA World Cup, played 8 June to 21 July 2030, about 44 days of football.
3 continentsThe first World Cup hosted across more than one confederation: Africa, Europe and South America.
100 yearsSince the first edition in 1930. A special centenary match and celebration will be held at Estadio Centenario in Montevideo.
North AfricaThe first World Cup there, and the second in Africa after South Africa 2010.
20 stadiumsCandidate venues across 17 cities: 6 in Morocco, 3 in Portugal and 11 in Spain. Final list still to be confirmed.
115,000Seats planned for the new Hassan II Stadium in Casablanca, the biggest on the list.
SpainThe first defending champions to later host the tournament, after winning their second World Cup in 2026.
64 teams?CONMEBOL announced a one-off 64-team expansion, but FIFA has not confirmed it. The team count is still to be announced.

Road to 2030

October 2020

Portugal and Spain announce they plan a joint bid.

March 2023

King Mohammed VI announces Morocco joins the bid.

October 2023

The FIFA Council backs the bid, with centenary matches in South America.

11 December 2024

FIFA confirms Morocco, Portugal and Spain as hosts, by acclamation.

2026

Spain win the World Cup and become the first defending champions to host the next one.

20 July 2026

CONMEBOL announces a 64-team tournament. FIFA has not confirmed it.

8 June 2030

Kickoff, with the final on 21 July.

Information as of 12 November 2026. Venues and format may still change.

Qualification

HostsMorocco, Portugal, Spain, Argentina, Paraguay and Uruguay have all qualified automatically.
UEFAA new format announced in May 2026: the 36 top-ranked countries play in three groups of 12, six games each, with the best of each group qualifying and play-offs for the rest. The other 18 countries form League 2.
CONCACAFThree rounds from September 2027 to 2029. The top two of each final group qualify, and a November 2029 play-in feeds the intercontinental play-offs.
